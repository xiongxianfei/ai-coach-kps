# Instructional Scaffolding

## Key takeaway

Scaffolding supports task components the learner cannot yet manage, with support adjusted to the task and evidence.

## Summary

This term is used in the cognitive-apprenticeship context described by Collins, Brown and Holum. A hint, a completed solution and an access accommodation are not automatically equivalent forms of assistance.

## Definition and distinction

The source describes suggestions or other support, sometimes with the instructor performing part of a task; fading removes support as the learner takes over. Accurate interpretation of difficulty matters.

## Constructed example

Instead of completing a learner's entire comparison, a coach supplies a table with empty headings. A later task uses fewer prompts. Whether that change is useful must be observed; a longer answer is not automatically a better scaffold.

## Scope and limits

This Concept does not require withholding all direct instruction or reducing help on a fixed timetable. An AI hint can be wrong. Access supports such as screen readers are not necessarily answer scaffolds to remove.
