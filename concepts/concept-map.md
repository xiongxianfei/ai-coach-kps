# Concept Map

## Key takeaway

A concept map makes relationships explicit rather than merely listing topics.

## Summary

In Novak and Cañas's account, labeled concept-to-concept links form meaningful propositions around a focus question. This definition does not make every topic outline a concept map.

## Definition and distinctions

Concept labels identify ideas; linking phrases state how they relate. A map can include hierarchical organization and cross-links between regions. Its organization depends on the focus question.

A directory tree organizes files. A topic outline groups headings. Neither necessarily states conceptual propositions. A prerequisite claim concerns learning conditions; it is not implied by a conceptual link.

## Constructed example

For the question "How can quantities be compared on a common basis?", the proposition `unit conversion -- preserves --> quantity` is an authored mathematical illustration. `Measurement, conversion, comparison` alone is just a topic list. Checking a label against a source does not establish that a learner understands the relation.

## Limits

No map is assumed exhaustive, uniquely correct, a direct picture of a mind, or a validated learning progression. A plausible link can be false. Source, scope and uncertainty remain necessary.
