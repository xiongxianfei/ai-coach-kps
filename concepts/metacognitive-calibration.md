# Metacognitive Calibration

## Key takeaway

Calibration compares a metacognitive judgment with a stated criterion, not with the fluency of an explanation.

## Summary

The traditional judgment–performance relationship and Pieschl's proposed broader account are distinguished. This Concept does not define a diagnostic score or learner trait.

## Definition and boundary

Traditional calibration concerns correspondence between a learner's judgments and performance criteria. Pieschl proposes considering regulation decisions and external task demands as well. These are related conceptualizations, not interchangeable measurement procedures.

## Constructed example

Before attempting a question, a learner records a confidence estimate. After preserving the response, the learner compares it with a justified criterion. A mismatch is a reason to investigate, not a stable diagnosis from one trial. Confidence after seeing the answer is a different observation.

## Scope and limits

A single item cannot establish a general calibration pattern. An AI's confidence is not the learner's metacognitive judgment. No scale, calibration statistic or psychological instrument is validated by this Concept.
