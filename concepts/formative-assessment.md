# Formative Assessment

## Key takeaway

Assessment becomes formative through its use in decisions about learning, not merely through collecting a score.

## Summary

Sadler's account particularly addresses judging quality against multiple criteria and developing the capacity to improve a response. The local coaching adaptation is described separately.

## Definition and distinctions

In the inspected account, the learner needs a conception of desired quality, the ability to compare current work with it, and moves for closing a gap. A comment that never informs action is not sufficient evidence of formative use.

## Constructed example

A learner's explanation is accurate but does not justify a causal claim. A stated rubric identifies that missing function; the learner revises and explains the revision. The output is not simply an AI replacement paragraph.

## Scope and limits

Sadler's theory is less directly concerned with tasks scored only right or wrong. A short arithmetic probe may support a local instructional choice but does not reproduce his complete model. Formative interpretation is not clinical diagnosis or an official proficiency judgment.
