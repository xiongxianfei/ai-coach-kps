# After Action Review

## Key takeaway

Compare the intended and actual course of a completed attempt, explain successes and difficulties cautiously, and choose a concrete adjustment to examine next time.

## Summary

After Action Review is a structured review of an event or project. Salem-Schatz, Ordin and Mittman's guide organizes it around expectations, events, successful actions and improvements. This Method adapts that team procedure to a learner's bounded attempt, with explicit separation of observations from causal guesses.

## Use when and prerequisites

Use this after a lesson, practice session or project episode when reviewing the process could change the next attempt. Obtain the original goal, criterion, work, feedback and assistance record. A useful review can examine success as well as failure. If all that is needed is correcting one known error, correct it directly.

## Procedural basis and rationale

The published overview asks reviewers to compare expectations with events, explain what worked and identify improvements. It emphasizes open discussion and preserving useful practices. The rationale for this adaptation is that an outcome alone does not tell a learner which action to repeat or change. A chronological record can expose a mismatch that a general impression of success conceals.

## Procedure for an individual attempt

1. Recover the intended outcome and conditions as they stood before the attempt. Do not silently replace the old goal with a more convenient one.
2. Reconstruct what happened from the work and record. Note relevant help, interruptions and deviations. Label recollections and missing information instead of filling gaps with an AI-generated narrative.
3. Compare the result with the criterion. Identify both an action worth retaining and a consequential difficulty, where the evidence supports them; do not invent one of each to fill a template.
4. Consider explanations. Separate a documented event from a proposed cause and ask what evidence could distinguish alternatives. Include task design and feedback quality, not only the learner's behavior.
5. Choose a feasible adjustment and the observation that would support keeping it. Record when to revisit it. If the evidence does not justify a change, retain the current approach provisionally or collect better evidence.

The individual record, competing-explanation check and follow-up decision are authored adaptations. Reviewing a process is not a controlled experiment that proves why an outcome occurred.

## Worked application

**Constructed example:** An adult planned to solve unfamiliar unit-price comparisons independently after a lesson. The session record instead shows that the AI displayed the division setup before each response. All final prices were correct.

The supported observation is successful calculation with supplied setups. Independent selection of the rate was not assessed. Possible explanations for the mismatch include an ambiguous help agreement or the AI supplying more than was requested; the record does not justify calling the learner dependent or unmotivated.

Keep the useful practice of checking units. Change the next assessment so the setup is withheld until the learner has responded, and record any actual leakage. A fresh comparison can show whether independent setup is possible under those conditions. It cannot establish that the review itself caused improvement.

If the learner deliberately requested a demonstration, treat that as an instructional choice. Revise the assessment plan instead of classifying the request as misconduct or failure.

## Output and validation

Produce a short account of intention, events, supported interpretation, unresolved explanations and the next adjustment or justified decision to retain the approach. Each claimed observation should be traceable to work or identified as recollection. A review is usable when the learner knows what to do differently and how to judge the next attempt; a long list of generic lessons is insufficient.

## AI role and limits

AI can organize the supplied record, ask about discrepancies and offer competing explanations. It must not invent missing events, attribute motives or flatter the learner instead of examining the criterion. The learner owns the interpretation and next decision. Keep completed reviews private.

A weekly review may aggregate several attempts and reprioritize goals; it need not repeat a full AAR for every session. [GROW Coaching](grow-coaching.md) helps choose future action, while this Method starts with an event that has already occurred. [Deliberate Practice Loop](deliberate-practice-loop.md) organizes the next targeted attempts rather than reviewing a whole episode.
