# Concept Mapping

## Key takeaway

Construct and revise propositions that answer a defined focus question.

## Summary

This summarizes Novak and Cañas's concept-mapping procedure. The result is a revisable representation; a separate instructional decision is needed to turn it into a learning plan.

## Inputs and prerequisites

A bounded question, material the mapmaker can examine, and a way to arrange concept labels and links. Paper or a text table is sufficient for the local representation; no particular software is required.

## Rationale

Making the linking relation explicit exposes what the proposed connection actually asserts.

## Procedure

1. Establish a focus question and a limited subject context.
2. List relevant concepts and arrange them from more inclusive to more specific where appropriate.
3. Connect concepts with linking phrases that make readable propositions.
4. Seek useful cross-links between regions, then revise the labels, links and layout.

## Output and validation

Read each proposition as a statement and check its relevance and defensibility. A table with source concept, linking phrase and target concept can preserve this information when a graphic is unavailable. The [Concept Map](../concepts/concept-map.md) object supplies the defining distinction.

## Constructed example

For comparing unit prices, consider `price divided by quantity -- gives --> unit price` and `unit conversion -- permits --> a common quantity basis`. These example statements still need context; the mapping operation does not prove a recommended order of instruction.

## Limits

This is not a prerequisite-discovery algorithm, a universal optimum for study, or a validated AI prompting protocol. Do not require the source's suggested concept counts in every task. An AI-generated map remains a proposal for inspection, not the learner's demonstrated knowledge.
