# Coherent lessons and the wider plan

## Key takeaway

Teach connected knowledge for a coherent lesson capability, then assess it at the agreed pace and adapt from actual responses.

## Summary

This reader guide connects lesson scope, flexible interaction pacing and the optional two-level learning-plan record. It distinguishes subject coverage, learning stages, lessons, assessment activities and actual observations. No fixed task duration, planning software, or validated mastery score is required.

## One continuing programme without document switching

The [master Practice](../practices/ai-assisted-learning-orchestration.md) puts the full procedure in one file. Define the overall target, identify provisional learning stages, scope a coherent lesson, teach connected content, invite clarification, present its assessment, wait for real answers, give brief checked feedback and carry out the next action. Within the existing learning agreement, begin the next lesson in the same reply when the evidence supports progressing; address a blocking gap first. Summarize each learning stage before moving beyond it. Preserve actual attempts separately from plan revisions, and resume from the current decision instead of restarting the whole procedure.

**Overall target → learning stage → lesson → task → attempt** is a learner-specific hierarchy. The master’s Stage1–Stage7 headings describe its reusable procedure, not seven mandatory subject modules. A lesson develops a focused capability through connected explanation and one or more tasks, presented together or in guided steps. A day can contain several lessons, or a lesson can span days; day labels remain scheduling choices. You may stay in the master; this guide and the specialist Practices provide optional record detail and alternative entry points, not required prerequisites.

## Explain knowledge within the lesson

The default delivery is **complete explanation and demonstration → all current lesson questions together → actual learner answers → combined feedback**. Prepare the actual teaching content before sending it, using a declared scope that keeps the necessary relationships together. Put the selected questions at the end of the same response, with no routine assessment interruptions between small ideas. “All” refers to the manageable current set, not an exhaustive bank; later clarification can address an important remaining gap. The learner can read at their own pace, consult the explanation for an application task, and ask for clarification. Such use is supported application, not evidence of unaided recall. Alternative pacing remains available; this is an authored default, not a claim of universal superiority.

The subject outline describes coverage; the learner's goal selects relevant knowledge. A stage organizes a broader capability, while a lesson focuses on a meaningful part. Select essential content and a checking criterion together: what must the learner understand to produce the intended response?

For new learning, orient to the question, explain the necessary terms and relationships, show why the result or procedure follows, and use a checked demonstration when useful. Connect any picture or equation to the same meaning in words. Include relevant boundaries and contrasts. Default to presenting the connected explanation before a short assessment set, with an opportunity to clarify anything unclear. Use a few short, complementary questions and accept brief answers. Request a reason where it reveals an essential relationship, without asking for a detailed explanation of every item. Each question should add useful evidence, such as interpretation, application or limits; there is no fixed question quota. A deeper follow-up is reserved for an important gap, not appended to every answer. Wait for the actual answers, review them together and revisit only remaining gaps. The [Instruction and Scaffolding Model](../models/instruction-and-scaffolding.md) supplies a worked lesson; the master Practice keeps the delivery steps inline.

These are flexible content responsibilities, not compulsory lesson headings or a requirement to use every technique. Choose direct explanation, examples, discussion, diagrams, demonstration or practice according to the goal and available evidence. A next action can develop a demonstrated strength or the agreed roadmap; it need not arise from an error. Follow the learner's pacing preference. Guided questions during teaching remain useful when requested or when a prerequisite or procedural decision needs checking; an interruption after each small idea is not required. Complete means sufficient for the lesson goal, not exhaustive coverage of a domain. For a baseline or retrieval task, ask before revealing the answer. Feedback and the next action follow the learner's actual response. Do not end routine feedback with a permission question: carry out the justified next action. By default, introduce and teach the next lesson when the learner is ready; clarification, practice, retrieval, synthesis or closing the stage may be more useful. If a misconception blocks that lesson, explain it and ask a focused check instead. Respect a requested pause, a material scope change or completion of the overall goal; do not manufacture a reply to keep the sequence moving. A lesson focus can be an ordinary note; no new required record field or lesson form is introduced.

The [question classification](../models/instruction-and-scaffolding.md#thinking-requested) separates thinking requested, answer format and purpose. Choose among its overlapping options according to the lesson; there is no requirement to use them all. Question fit matters more than wording length. Connect each question to the lesson capability, a stated prerequisite or a planned review; make the task and sufficient response clear. Adjust challenge and answer effort separately, and consider the burden of the whole set. Clarify an unclear prompt, remove incidental demands when the task is overwhelming, or choose a useful application when the distinction is already demonstrated. Use actual responses and the learner's reported effort to adapt; do not infer ability or motivation from silence or brevity. The master's [question-selection procedure](../practices/ai-assisted-learning-orchestration.md#stage3-select-one-current-learning-task) gives the operational guidance without a fixed question count or difficulty ladder.

The [connected lesson example](WORKED-EXAMPLE.md#connected-lesson-example) shows how a specific response motivates feedback and the next lesson, followed by worked reasoning, a changed condition, complementary application tasks and a learning-note update. It illustrates teaching decisions rather than prescribing a lesson layout or claiming learner results.

## Summarize each learning stage

At the end of a learning stage, give a readable synthesis before starting the next stage. Explain the central mechanism and how the ideas connect, include one checked overview picture by default, and state what the learner's actual answers support, what remains open and why the next stage follows. Keep the essential explanation in text too and adapt the representation to learner preferences and access needs. These are content responsibilities, not a compulsory form or a new examination.

Reuse a suitable subject illustration or create one with available tools, checking its labels, relations and examples before embedding it. Additional pictures are optional. If a picture is unavailable, provide the text summary and state that limitation without claiming an image exists. A diagram of knowledge does not demonstrate learner competence.

An introductory pass, provisional move forward and demonstrated stage capability are different conclusions. Use the description supported by the observations; practical performance and delayed retention remain untested until observed. An existing adequate response can support the decision without a duplicate stage test. When continuing is appropriate, follow the summary with the next stage's purpose and first lesson in the same reply, then wait for answers.

Keep learner summaries in numbered files under a private `stages/` folder, with pictures embedded beside the text. The overview retains the goal, initial assessment under Starting point, roadmap, and current assessment and next action under Where I am now. All completed learner records stay outside public repositories. A stage summary can link to earlier attempts while preserving original and assisted responses separately. The public subject repository owns reusable explanations and illustrations; its body-of-knowledge outline records coverage, not this learner's progress. The AI coach Practice owns the summarizing procedure. See [private record placement](learning-notes.md#stage-summaries-and-private-storage) for a minimal layout. The copyable templates are optional scaffolds; no software schema or tracking system is required.

## Maintain the learning notes

The [learning-notes guide](learning-notes.md) owns the file layout and evidence distinctions. Copy the [overview](../templates/learning-notes/learning-notes.md) and [stage](../templates/learning-notes/stages/01-stage-name.md) templates into a private location. The default is `learning-notes.md` plus numbered files in `stages/`, with the initial assessment inside the overview. Record maintenance supports the lesson; it does not require another question or confirmation at every step.

| Occasion | Useful update |
|---|---|
| Begin | Record the goal and preferences, preserve self-report and actual baseline evidence distinctly under Starting point, and mark unobserved capabilities unknown. Establish a provisional current assessment from the available evidence. |
| Teach and receive a reply | Keep a provisional lesson outline at the top of the current stage, followed by important learner-written notes, useful explanations and meaningful responses. Preserve the original answer, actual assistance and checked feedback separately; skip routine conversational filler. |
| Evidence changes a conclusion | Update the dated current assessment with a link to the observation and its conditions. Preserve a superseded consequential judgment with the reason for correction in the stage notes. |
| Finish or pause a stage | Add the connected summary and overview picture, identify supported understanding and open gaps, then update the roadmap, current assessment and next action. |
| Resume | Read the overview, current stage and cited evidence needed for the pending decision. Ask only for missing context or changed circumstances; do not restart the baseline automatically. |
| Change the goal or route | Record the changed scope and reason, retain the original assessment and earlier evidence, and keep existing stage identifiers stable. If stage order changes, update numeric filenames and affected links together. |
| Find a reusable knowledge gap | Check the proposed explanation, improve the owning knowledge file within the authorized scope, and link back from the note without publishing personal records. |

The current assessment summarizes available evidence; it does not require a new test at every update. Assisted success cannot silently become independent performance, and a planned later check remains a plan. When no private storage is available, provide a compact update for the learner to save without claiming persistent memory or completed file writes.

## Default adaptive roadmap for a new subject

The self-contained master Practice supplies the [six-step process](../practices/ai-assisted-learning-orchestration.md#default-planning-handoff) and its [connections to knowledge domains](../practices/ai-assisted-learning-orchestration.md#how-knowledge-domains-support-the-process). This optional guide summarizes the handoff to a learning task. Do **not** start with a detailed multiweek list of daily questions.

| Step | What the learner and coach decide | Keep distinct |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Learner starting point | Short self-report on goals, prior experience and constraints; optionally one checked response collected **before instruction** | Self-reported confidence versus observed competence; unknown is not failed |
| 2. Subject areas or subsystems | A compact, source-grounded panorama: each candidate area's central question, boundary, an example and important overlaps | Academic explanatory perspectives versus arbitrary syllabus chapters |
| 3. High-level stages | A few provisional capabilities, combining areas as useful, with candidate checkpoints and reasoned prerequisites | Subject-map relationships versus instructional prerequisites |
| 4. Lesson and assessment | Coherent capability, connected content and a current activity or question set with checked criteria, permitted help and stop/split rule | Lesson scope versus interaction pace and day labels |
| 5. Teach and assess | Teach the connected lesson, invite clarification, present questions at the agreed pace, wait, then review answers together; preserve original responses and actual help | Supplied explanation versus learner-generated evidence; baseline and retrieval questions precede answer teaching |
| 6. Adapt | Give feedback and begin the next lesson or repair a blocking gap; summarize at stage boundaries and revise the roadmap when warranted | Task success versus independent stage-level or retained learning |

For English, a panorama might distinguish phonetics, phonology, syntax, semantics and pragmatics by their explanatory questions, while recognizing the cross-cutting role of prosody. A learner's proposed stage could combine syntax and semantics to explain one sentence; there is no one-to-one rule assigning each linguistic area its own week. Show the roadmap for orientation, then prepare **the current lesson and its assessment**. Keep later lessons provisional; the current assessment can include several related questions.

## Scope before questions

Choose a coherent lesson capability first, then the activity or questions that can reveal it. A focused diagnostic, retrieval task or direct explanation can still be used on its own.

## Find the next task

1. **Locate the goal.** For a new subject, use a brief panoramic orientation and a goal-specific concept map to locate one meaningful capability; reuse an existing checked map if available. For a well-defined small question, begin directly and backfill the map only if it helps. The panorama is context, not a compulsory preliminary study session.
2. **Choose the lesson capability and evidence.** Ask what the learner should explain or do, which ideas must connect, and what responses would demonstrate that capability. Select complementary questions or an integrated activity. Do not make each definition a separate lesson; split when the scope genuinely exceeds the learner's preparation or constraints.
3. **State the task boundary.** Agree on the input or reference, response to produce, permissible answer assistance and access supports, criterion for checking, and conditions for stopping or splitting. A deadline or time box is optional and locally chosen, not an empirically guaranteed optimal duration.
4. **Attempt before feedback when appropriate.** Keep the original response and the actual assistance. A full explanation or worked example is legitimate when prerequisites are absent or the learner requests it, but it is not an independent attempt.
5. **Review and decide.** Compare the available responses with their criteria, explain common misunderstandings together, and leave unanswered parts unobserved. Judge lesson completion against the full agreed capability, not a single correct sub-answer. Choose *continue*, *revisit*, *split*, *increase the challenge*, *defer*, or *stop*, and note the reason. Never mark delayed retention or transfer as observed merely because the next task is planned.

## A usable one task card

| Field | What to write |
|---|---|
| Goal and location | What larger capability and map region this task serves, if known |
| Next task | A bounded activity or related question set within a defined, authorized scope |
| Observable output | The response, explanation or artifact the learner will produce |
| Checking criterion | What evidence would support or challenge success on **this** task |
| Assistance boundary | Allowed tools, answer hints, source access and accommodations |
| Stop or split rule | When to check, clarify, break down, defer or end the task |
| Record link | Optional private plan version, learning-stage ID and task ID |

For a single small task without a formal curriculum, only the task, output, criterion, assistance boundary and next decision are necessary. The JSON planning record is optional; a private note is sufficient.

## Constructed first day examples

**Introductory machine learning.** Given a checked introductory definition and a constructed example of training and test data, explain in your own words why low training error does not guarantee good performance on unseen data. **Output:** one explanation and a contrasting counterexample. **Criterion:** separate observed training performance from generalization, noting that unseen data may differ. **Next:** check one misunderstanding or choose a new application; do not infer broad ML mastery.

**Authorized security laboratory.** Given a lab host explicitly within your permission and one already collected service observation, identify what the observation shows, what it does *not* establish, and **one justified next investigation question**. **Output:** a short observation–hypothesis–question record. **Criterion:** the conclusion follows from evidence rather than an assumed vulnerability. **Next:** revisit service fundamentals or select a different scoped observation. Do not invent scanning results or provide an exploitation chain. Follow the lab provider's rules for AI assistance; do not use AI where an examination prohibits it. "Day 1" is an example label, not a universal curriculum duration.

Both examples are invented instructional designs, not completed learner results or evaluated AI interventions.

## Connect the five Practices

- [Concept Mapping and Instructional Sequencing](../practices/concept-mapping-and-instructional-sequencing-with-ai.md) chooses the next task **from** a tentative staged plan; it owns revisions to that plan.
- [Self-Regulated Learning](../practices/self-regulated-learning-with-ai.md) carries out the current task and preserves the learner's actual response.
- [Formative Assessment](../practices/formative-assessment-with-ai.md) investigates **one** observed discrepancy and proposes a justified adjustment.
- [Retrieval and Distributed Practice](../practices/retrieval-and-distributed-practice-with-ai.md) conducts a bounded recall task and separately plans later review.
- [Evaluation](../practices/evaluation-of-ai-assisted-learning.md) interprets a **specified** learning claim without converting one success into a mastery certificate.

Do not require all five for every task. No AI prompt by itself produces persistent memory, automatic reminders, source verification, correct grading or learner agreement.

## Use and evidence limits

These steps are KPS-specific design choices informed by the existing academically source-aligned library, not findings that every learner or subject benefits from identical task sizes. More substantial integration tasks are sometimes needed to assess relationships across topics. Task completion and independent, delayed learning remain distinct. Store actual records privately; do not publish learner data or fabricate observations.

## Optional two level learning plan

A plain private table is sufficient; the structured JSON schema is optional and cannot certify learning.

## Begin with the three artifacts

| Artifact | Minimum useful information | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Subject panorama | Boundary, major regions, purposes, selected relations, source limits and omissions | Complete disciplinary coverage or understanding |
| Goal concept map | One focus question, selected concepts, explicit propositions and links back to the panorama | Which concept must be learned first |
| Adaptive stage plan | Capability, concepts, activity, proposed prerequisites, allowed assistance, check, criterion and revision trigger | An observed learning outcome or universal progression |

The prerequisite register is a set of task-sensitive hypotheses, not a fourth knowledge level. Label a relation as required for this task, helpful, or corequisite only with a rationale and a way to probe it. Even a supported declaration must state the evidence and scope; the checker cannot verify it.

## Use the optional template

1. Copy [the blank template](../data/two-level-learning-plan-template.json) outside the public repository. Use `record_kind: template` while fields are intentionally empty; a template pass does not mean a usable plan exists.
2. Inspect [the constructed example](../data/two-level-learning-plan-example.json). It demonstrates syntax and hypothetical design only. No source review, learner agreement or performance is invented by the example.
3. Fill the panorama before zooming to the goal map. Existing checked panoramas can be reused; keep the versions and scope visible. Select `proposed_plan` when the records contain a usable proposal, or `constructed_example` for explicitly invented demonstration data.
4. Use stable region, concept and learning-stage IDs. `order` is a replaceable display position, not the stage identity or proof of developmental necessity. A stage ID such as `normalize` remains unchanged when reordered. This differs from the repository Practice's navigable StageN headings.
5. Keep the learner's actual response in a separate [session record](learning-notes.md). Refer to it privately in the prerequisite evidence or revision history rather than copying it into the public knowledge base.

## Choose one current task

A learning plan can contain several provisional stages while selecting **one current activity**, which may be a short assessment set. The optional record retains its existing single `next_task` object; its task and criterion text can contain numbered questions and corresponding criteria. This format does not impose a one-question teaching rule. Use the optional `next_task` object to specify an `id`, `stage_id`, `task`, `observable_output`, `criterion`, `allowed_resources`, `assistance_boundary` and `stop_rule`. This is a proposed activity, not a learner response or a validated progression. `null` means no next task is selected yet; prior plans without this optional field remain readable by the checker. The ID references a stable learning-stage ID, not the navigable `StageN` headings of a KPS Practice. A day label is optional and never a performance claim.

Choose evidence that covers the lesson capability rather than minimizing each question. If important relationships need to be applied together, the next task may be integrative. A ready learner may bypass redundant preliminary episodes with supporting evidence. See [the scoping steps](#find-the-next-task); retain the original map, plan and revision history rather than overwriting earlier decisions.

## Field interpretation

`sources` records citations, locators and inspected/uninspected/constructed status. Source references on nodes or propositions support the content declaration, not learner competence. `source_supported` requires a declared inspected source; a source declaration does not prove that inspection occurred.

`panorama.regions` supplies the broad view, while `goal.panorama_region_ids` and `goal_map.concepts[].panorama_region_ids` preserve the zoom relationship. `goal_map.relations` stores source concept, linking phrase and target concept. Nodes alone are not the intended concept map. Source-supported, unverified and constructed propositions remain distinguishable.

`prerequisite_hypotheses` names concepts, kind, proposed/supported/rejected status, rationale, probe and evidence references. Unsupported hypotheses may remain proposed. Rejected prerequisites cannot remain in a stage's prerequisite list. Evidence references are descriptions of private records, not files the checker uploads or opens.

`learning_stages` provides target capability, concepts, activities, allowed instructional help, a separately specified check and an adaptation trigger. `next_task` selects at most one current proposed task from those stages; it never stores an observed answer. `requires_stage_ids` is reserved for a chosen required-before ordering of learning episodes. It needs its own rationale and may be revised; conceptual map edges are never converted automatically to stage dependencies. Related concepts may form cycles, but contradictory strict stage ordering must be repaired or combined into a corequisite episode.

`learner_agreement` remains `not_recorded` until actual agreement exists. `agreed` needs `agreement_evidence`; neither the assistant nor a test should fabricate it. `revision_log` states versions, affected stage IDs, the change and reason. A source correction or learner choice can justify a revision without inventing a performance result; a performance-based revision requires an actual observation reference. Retired or split stage IDs stay in the history instead of being repurposed.

## Adaptation from evidence

| Observation or choice | Planning response |
|---|---|
| Prior capability is demonstrated appropriately | Consider omitting redundant teaching; retain the observation |
| A relationship cannot be explained or used | Add a relational example or integration task |
| An answer is correct only after help | Record assistance and plan a new independent check |
| A required-before hypothesis is contradicted | Revise that hypothesis and the stages that relied on it |
| A source or map claim is erroneous | Correct the representation before interpreting learner performance |
| Goal, workload or interest changes | Re-scope or defer with learner agreement, not an invented failure |

A learner can pause, request a direct explanation or decline a check. There is no universally required number of regions, concepts, stages, attempts or minutes. Later retention and changed-context checks remain planned until actually performed.

## Read only checks

```bash
python3 tools/check_learning_plan.py data/two-level-learning-plan-template.json
python3 tools/check_learning_plan.py data/two-level-learning-plan-example.json
```

The checker validates the declared format, links between levels, proposition fields, references, prerequisites, stage ordering and revision declarations. It accepts conceptual cycles but rejects contradictory required-before stage cycles. It does not write, generate stages, fetch sources, inspect private referenced records, infer a learner state, schedule sessions or classify mastery. A successful check is structural only.

The source-aligned [Concept Map](../concepts/concept-map.md) and [Concept Mapping](../methods/concept-mapping.md) objects explain the scholarly representation and procedure. This record schema and the two-level coaching sequence are authored design choices. Preserve source limitations and keep private content outside public version control. These examples are constructed; no real learning results are stored here.
