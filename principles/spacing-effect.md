# Spacing Effect

## Key takeaway

Learning episodes separated in time can affect later retention, with useful gaps depending on the retention goal.

## Summary

The inspected spacing research is bounded to its verbal and fact-learning tasks. It supports considering time explicitly, not promising an optimal universal calendar.

## Statement

The selected research relates distribution of practice to retention. Cepeda and colleagues' temporal-ridgeline abstract reports that a favorable interstudy gap depends on the eventual test delay.

## Explanation and boundary

Spacing and retrieval are distinct: one concerns temporal arrangement, the other the learning activity. Interleaving categories is a further design dimension, not a synonym for either. More elapsed time is not automatically better.

## Constructed application

An adult planning to use a concept next month can choose later review opportunities and record actual intervals. Scheduling is a revisable local choice; missing a planned review is not an observed retrieval failure.

## Limits

Abstract-only access does not support extracting a detailed individualized algorithm. The learner's calendar, item difficulty and changing competence are not supplied by the research title.
