# Testing Effect

## Key takeaway

Retrieval can contribute to later retention rather than only measure current recall.

## Summary

The selected experiments support a condition-specific relationship. The published technique and the design of an AI review session must remain separate.

## Statement

Karpicke and Blunt found later benefits for a study–recall–restudy treatment under their reported comparisons. This is not a rule that all testing is superior to every other learning activity.

## Explanation and evidence boundary

Recall is one component of the treatment. Mayrhofer and colleagues changed the comparison, including additional memorization, and obtained different outcome-specific findings. Neither study alone establishes general superiority or validates this package's coaching sequence.

## Constructed application

A learner can record an answer before opening reference material, then inspect omissions. A later check remains a separate event. The record can discriminate recalled material from copied correction, but cannot isolate the causal contribution of retrieval without a suitable comparison.

## Limits

Material, prior learning, feedback, opportunity to restudy and outcome timing matter. Failed recall without adequate instruction is not automatically a desirable learning condition.
