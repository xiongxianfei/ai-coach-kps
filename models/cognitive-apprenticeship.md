# Cognitive Apprenticeship

## Key takeaway

The published framework relates contextual expertise, guided participation and increasingly independent activity.

## Summary

This file summarizes an explanatory subset of Collins, Brown and Holum's framework. It is not a new six-step KPS theory or evidence that a language model has expert cognition.

## Purpose and representation

| Dimension | Source components |
|---|---|
| Content | Domain knowledge, heuristic strategies, control strategies, learning strategies |
| Method | Modeling, coaching, scaffolding, articulation, reflection, exploration |
| Sequencing | Increasing complexity and diversity, global skills before local skills |
| Sociology | Situated learning, community, motivation and cooperation |

The dimensions interact; the six methods are not an obligatory sequence of six sessions.

## Constructed interpretation

In a small programming task, a verified expert solution can be explained, attempted with limited support and compared with the learner's own solution. The specific hint timing, AI instructions and success criteria belong in a Practice. These choices do not become published theory because the table above is cited.

## Assumptions and limits

The source describes knowledgeable instruction and context. A model-generated rationale is not a transcript of hidden reasoning or proof of expertise. Social and motivational aspects are not exhausted by a chatbot exchange.

## Evaluation

Check whether the application actually includes the claimed relationships and preserves the source's boundaries. A diagram or successful model response is not evidence that an adult has learned.
