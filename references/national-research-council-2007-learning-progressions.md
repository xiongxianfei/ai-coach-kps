# Learning Progressions in Taking Science to School

## Key takeaway

A proposed order of lessons is not automatically a researched learning progression.

## Summary

This source is used to qualify terminology, not to prescribe a curriculum for independent adults using AI.

## Publication identity

National Research Council. 2007. *Taking Science to School: Learning and Teaching Science in Grades K-8*. Chapter 8, Learning Progressions. Washington, DC: The National Academies Press. DOI: 10.17226/11625. [Official chapter](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11625/chapter/10).

## Inspected contribution

The chapter describes progressively more sophisticated understanding over extended periods in school science. It calls for research-grounded sequences around core ideas and recognizes multiple, interacting paths. Proposed progressions guide investigation rather than acquire validation merely by being written down.

## Applicability and limits

This is a K–8 science research context, not evidence for a particular adult AI-coaching sequence. Here it supports a terminology boundary: call our learner-specific output a provisional instructional sequence. Domain-specific claims about its effectiveness still require evidence.

## Nonclaims

No empirical validation of our plan, universal prerequisite order, learner result, optimal number of stages, or exact age-based developmental path is inferred. No chapter figures or full text are redistributed.
